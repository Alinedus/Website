LOOP → LINE

One idea carries the whole site. Act I is a closed circuit an architect cannot escape. At the midpoint the circuit is cut and pulled straight — and the straight line it becomes is the logo. Act II walks that line from intent to execution.

The name already contains the argument. alined — a line. The shortest distance between two points. We are not decorating that tagline; we are animating it literally, once, at the exact centre of the page.

01  /  what's actually in the brand files

The logo is not a wordmark. It's a construction system.

I opened the SVGs. Every letter of alined is built from ∅32 dots sitting on a 32-unit grid inside a 720 viewBox, joined by small tangent fillets. The Primary Logo file even carries its own drafting grid, drawn in scarlet at 10-unit spacing. This isn't a font that was set — it's a thing that was drafted. Which means the site doesn't need a decorative visual language invented for it. It already has one: points, snapping, and the lines between them.

Insight 01 — the one that unlocks the whole thing

The d is already a person standing next to their work.

A lowercase d is a vertical stem with a bowl beside it. In your submark the scarlet dot floats detached, exactly two grid cells above the stem. Head. Body. And the bowl on the left is the room he drew. Your stick man isn't an illustration we bolt on — he is the logo, disassembled. Which means the site can open by taking the logo apart and close by putting it back together, and both moves are structurally true rather than decorative.

Insight 02 — the shape of Act I

The loop should be a Penrose staircase, not a circle.

A ring reads as "cycle" and stops there. An impossible staircase reads as a drawing that cannot be built — which is the architect's actual problem, told as an architecture joke. They climb every step, gain zero elevation, and arrive back at the bottom. Every architect who lands on this page gets it in half a second without reading a word. And it gives Act I a genuinely spectacular 3D object to orbit.

Two families, ranged hard: Geist Sans 400–800 with the variable axis animated on scroll, and Geist Mono for every HUD, counter and station label. Both variable, both installed. That's the full type system — nothing else is needed.

Open question — two extra fonts arrived

The bundle also contained DM Sans and Dela Gothic One. Neither is in the brand book, and Dela Gothic One in particular is a heavy blocky display face that would pull the site somewhere quite different from Geist. If they came along by accident with the Google Fonts download, I'll ignore them. If your founder wants Dela Gothic One as the display face, say so — it's a real direction, but it changes the type system and I'd want to re-cut the specimen above before P0.

Motion language — three rules, applied everywhere

Things snap, they don't float

Elements arrive at 32-unit grid positions with expo.out and a one-frame overshoot. Nothing eases lazily into place. The brand is a drafting tool — it has magnetism.

Act I is heavy, Act II is instant

Same easing curves, opposite durations. Act I moves take 600–900ms and resist. After the cut, everything resolves in 120–200ms. The site's own responsiveness is the argument.

Scarlet only moves once per scene

If two red things animate at once, neither is the head. One scarlet event per beat, maximum. It is the eye's anchor for the entire 100% of scroll.

04  /  what it's built with

The stack, with honest verdicts.

This is the combination every Awwwards-tier scroll site is actually running in 2026. Where something popular isn't right for this project I've said so rather than padding the list.

core

GSAP 3.13+

ScrollTrigger, MotionPath, DrawSVG, MorphSVG, SplitText, Observer. Since Webflow bought GreenSock the entire toolset — including every formerly paid plugin — is free for commercial use. DrawSVG draws the circuit; MorphSVG does sketch→plan; MotionPath walks him around the loop.

core

Three.js r170+

The Penrose circuit, the camera on a CatmullRomCurve3, the plan-to-3D extrusion, the final building. Vanilla — no React Three Fiber. A reconciler fights heavily imperative scroll timelines and buys us nothing on a single-page site.

core

Lenis 1.x

Smooth scroll. Chosen over ScrollSmoother specifically for its prevent API — we need it for the pinned horizontal section in Act II, and to make the scroll go heavy at scene 05.

core

Vite + TypeScript

No framework. The site has one page, no data, no routing. Every dependency added past this point is a frame we don't get back.

core

Custom GLSL

Three shaders, that's all: the drafting grid, the render-noise-resolve at station RENDER, and the dot-cloud morph used at the cut. Written by hand — no post-processing library.

verdict

The stick man procedural, not modelled

A 14-point rig — head, neck, shoulders, elbows, hands, hip, knees, feet — with lines between. A few KB, fully scroll-drivable, and literally made of the logo's dots. A rigged 3D character or a Rive file would both be heavier and less on-brand. This is the call I'd defend hardest.

optional

Blender + MCP, phase 2

Only needed for the finished hero building at EXECUTION. Everything else is procedural, because a GLB can't animate its own extrusion out of a floor plan — which is the whole point of station MODEL. Blender MCP is real and handles hard-surface architecture well, but it is not a blocker for launch.

skipping

Rive / Lottie / Spline

Rive's state machines are excellent, and if the character stops working after a first pass it's the fallback. But a second canvas over the WebGL one breaks the trick that he's made of the same dots as the scene. Spline is too heavy for what we'd get.

Go longer than v1 said

I planned 9–11 viewports. Lando runs 15.1 with 21 canvases and holds attention fine. Revising the target to 12–15 — the story we have wants the room, particularly Act I's laps.

Real scroll, not hijacked

Obys and Resn both collapse the document to one viewport and virtualise scrolling. We follow Lando instead: a genuinely long document. Our whole spine is scroll position = story position, and that needs real depth for ScrollTrigger and the progress line to mean anything.

One face, ranged hard

None of the three pairs a display face with a body face in the conventional way — Obys ships a proprietary face and uses it for everything. Geist across both cuts is not a limitation, it is what these sites actually do.

The Obys preloader

I caught Obys mid-load: black screen, a small collage, and a percentage counter reading 82. That is scene 00 almost exactly as written. Confirms the counter idiom rather than making it feel borrowed — everyone in this tier does it, and ours draws the logo while it counts.
